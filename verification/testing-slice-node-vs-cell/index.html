      Node vs cell slices

      Node-centred against cell-centred slices

      ComponentReading of FDS slices at the sampling height: K, the walking-speed factor, the gas and heat FED rates (#231)
      LevelFDS case, read with pyFDS-Evac’s own samplers and rate functions; no evacuation is run
      Assetassets/slice_node_vs_cell/slice_node_vs_cell.fds, scripts/verification/slice_node_vs_cell.py
      Expected value fromnone: this is a measurement of a difference, not a pass/fail check
      StatusMeasured with FDS 6.10.1. It quantifies how far the two readings are apart; it does not decide which one is right

      The two readings

      FDS+Evac reads the cell-centred mass fractions and density of the fire cell that holds the centre of each evacuation-grid cell, at HUMAN_SMOKE_HEIGHT (default 1.6 m): GET_FIRE_CONDITIONS takes ZZ and RHO of the cell (II, JJ, KK), with KK = FLOOR(...) of the nose height (evac.f90 at c9da70d7a, GET_FIRE_CONDITIONS and the HUMAN_GRID setup above it).

      pyFDS-Evac reads the value position of a slice nearest the agent, at the frame nearest the time (FDS slice sampling). A default &SLCF slice is node-centred: FDS writes one value per mesh node, averaged from the cells around it (FDS User’s Guide, &SLCF). A CELL_CENTERED=T slice holds the unaveraged cell values. Near a sharp gradient (a plume edge, a layer interface) the averaged node value differs from the cell value.

      Setup

      One compartment, 6 × 6 × 3 m, adiabatic walls, a 0.6 × 0.6 m opening in the x = 0 wall and a 0.4 × 0.4 m propane burner (160 kW after a 10 s ramp, SOOT_YIELD 0.05, CO_YIELD 0.04) at the centre. The grid is 0.2 m in x and y and 0.1 m in z, so z = 1.6 m is a node plane and the cell centres next to it are at 1.55 and 1.65 m. The hot layer fills the room from the ceiling and crosses 1.6 m while the plume stands in the middle: over the 120 s the mean temperature on the plane rises from 20 °C to about 100 °C and K from 0 to about 1.9 1/m. FDS 6.10.1 (FDS-6.10.1-0-g12efa16-release), OpenMP with four threads, 10 min wall time on a shared machine. The output is in the data store under fds-evac-data/slice_node_vs_cell.

      At z = 1.6 m the deck writes SOOT EXTINCTION COEFFICIENT, the VOLUME FRACTION of CO, CO2 and O2, and TEMPERATURE, as a default node-centred &SLCF and as CELL_CENTERED=T slices at 1.55 m and 1.65 m. The cell whose lower face is 1.6 m (centre 1.65 m) is the one FDS+Evac’s FLOOR indexing gives; the cell below (1.55 m) is the other candidate, because a nose height that falls exactly on a cell face is decided by rounding.

      Analysis

      scripts/verification/slice_node_vs_cell.py builds pyFDS-Evac’s own readers on each family (SliceFieldSampler, ExtinctionField with SmokeSpeedModel and the default Lund law, FdsFedField with default_fed_rate_per_minute, FdsHeatField with default_heat_fed_rate_per_minute) and reads every output frame (61 frames, 2 s apart) at every fire cell of the plane (900):

      • node at the cell centre, which is exactly halfway between two nodes per axis and so a tie that the sampler gives to the lower node; and at the four quadrant points of the cell, each nearest to a different corner node, as agents spread over the cell would read;
      • cell at the cell centre of the 1.65 m slice, of the 1.55 m slice, and their mean, which isolates the horizontal averaging of the node value from the half-cell vertical offset.

      The interface zone is where a sharp gradient meets the plane. A cell is sharp at a frame when the cell-centred temperature (mean of the two layers) steps by at least 10 % of the frame’s range across a face to an in-plane neighbour or between the two layers, and the range is at least 5 K; the zone is the sharp cells dilated by one cell. It holds 5.5 % of the cell-frames and is mostly the plume edge; the layer crossing the plane adds the frames in which the two layers differ. Relative differences use the samples where either reading exceeds a floor (K 1e-3 1/m, FED rate 1e-5 1/min, heat rate 1e-6 1/min), with |cell| as the denominator.

      The doses are within 5 % of the cell dose except at the plume edge (2.7, 2.7) m, where the node reading spans −5 % to +89 % depending on where in the cell the agent stands. The heat FED dose shows the same pattern more strongly, because it is a steep function of temperature: at (2.7, 2.7) m the node dose is 0.043 (centre) and 0.14 (quadrant mean, range 0.043 to 0.36) against 0.069 for the cell, and at (3.5, 3.3) m 0.068 against 0.040. All doses in this deck stay far below 1, so none of the differences reaches an incapacitation threshold.

      What it means for a comparison with FDS+Evac

      • Away from gradients the two readings agree to a few per cent in K and a few tenths of a per cent in the speed factor. A comparison of evacuation times with FDS+Evac in a smoke layer that is horizontally uniform at 1.6 m is not affected by the slice centring at this level.
      • At a plume edge or while a layer crosses the plane the node value is an average of up to eight cells, so K, T and the FED rates differ by tens of per cent in the median and by factors at the P95. The speed factor is bounded by the Lund law (floor 0.1, factor 1 at K = 0), so its absolute difference stays below 0.15 here, but the heat dose and the gas dose are steep functions of T and CO and are not.
      • The sign of the difference depends on where the agent stands relative to the gradient: at the plume edge (2.7, 2.7) m the node reading gives 0.043 to 0.36 for the heat dose against 0.069 for the cell, so the node value is both lower and higher than the cell value within one cell.
      • This quantifies a difference. It does not decide which reading is right: the cell value is what FDS+Evac uses and what FDS solved for, the node value is a smoothed field of the same solution. One deck, one grid (0.2 m by 0.2 m by 0.1 m) and one fuel are measured; a finer grid shrinks the averaging distance, a coarser one grows it.

      CELL_CENTERED=T as an option

      pyFDS-Evac reads cell-centred slices: the sampler takes the cell centres of a CELL_CENTERED=T slice as value positions, and the slice height is the centre of the cell that holds the requested z (FDS slice sampling). A deck that writes only cell-centred slices at the sampling height therefore gives the FDS+Evac reading with no change to pyFDS-Evac. When one case holds both families at similar heights, the run reads the horizontal slice whose height is nearest the requested one (the first declared on a tie); there is no option to prefer cell-centred slices, and none was added. To compare, write the cell-centred slice at the height you want and the node-centred ones elsewhere, or run twice with the slices in separate decks.

      fds slice_node_vs_cell.fds        # FDS 6.10.1; output to <DATA>

SLICE_NODE_VS_CELL_DATA=<DATA> pytest tests/verification/test_slice_node_vs_cell.py
