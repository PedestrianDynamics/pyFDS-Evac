      NIST TN 1822 status

      Status against NIST TN 1822

      NIST Technical Note 1822 (Ronchi, Kuligowski, Reneke, Peacock and Nilsson, 2013, The process of verification and validation of building fire evacuation models) suggests 17 verification tests (Verif.1.1 to Verif.5.2, Table 1 of the report) and, separately, validation tests. The table below gives the status of each verification test for pyFDS-Evac. The test list and the descriptions are from the report itself. Everything on this page is verification: it checks that the code reproduces the correlation or hand calculation it implements. None of it is validation against observed evacuations. The report’s own prose numbers some tests differently from its Table 1 (it calls the counter-flow test Verif.2.5 in one paragraph); this page follows Table 1.

      Six NIST tests overlap an ISO 20414 test: Verif.2.5 and ISO 18, Verif.2.6 and ISO 19, Verif.3.1 and ISO 8, Verif.3.3 and ISO 16, Verif.4.1 and ISO 9, Verif.5.2 and ISO 11. Where the geometry and the timing are the same, one test carries both markers. Where they differ, the table says so, and for Verif.2.5 and Verif.4.1 a separate test runs NIST’s own setup.

      To run the NIST TN 1822 cases on your machine (the rows marked run below), use uv run pytest -m "nist_tn1822 and not external_data"; the same command runs in the “NIST TN 1822” workflow on every push and pull request to main.

      “Run” means that a maintained test reproduces the setup and checks the expected result against a value computed by hand. “Inherited” means that the component is JuPedSim’s movement model, which pyFDS-Evac does not change and has not rerun. “Out of scope” means that pyFDS-Evac does not model the component. “Not yet run” means that the component belongs to pyFDS-Evac, or is configured through it, but no test reproduces the NIST setup. Of the 17 tests, 6 are run, 4 are inherited, 5 are out of scope and 2 are not yet run.

      The three fire–people interaction tests

      NIST suggests three tests of components that pyFDS-Evac adds to JuPedSim: Verif.2.5, Verif.2.6 and Verif.4.1. JuPedSim alone cannot pass them: its own suite runs Verif.2.5 and Verif.2.6 against adapters inside the test and cannot run Verif.4.1, because it has no runtime exit closure (JuPedSim V&V suite, standards/nist/README.md at commit 41ea112: Verif.2.5 and 2.6 “test-local”, Verif.4.1 “pending”, “no public runtime exit closure”).

      Verif.2.5 is NIST’s corridor at K = 1.0 1/m and 1.25 m/s, which is the ISO 18 corridor (assets/ISO-table21). test_nist_verif_2_5_reduced_visibility.py walks it in clear air and under both smoke-speed laws and compares the time over a 60 m stretch (x = −30 m to +30 m, frame resolution 0.1 s) with the hand calculation. Clear air: 60 / 1.25 = 48.0 s. Frantzich–Nilsson: f = 1 − 0.057 / 0.706 = 0.919263, w = 1.149079 m/s, 60 / w = 52.215 s. Fridolf with A = 2: visibility 2 / 1 = 2 m, w = 1.25 − 0.34 (3 − 2) = 0.91 m/s, 60 / w = 65.934 s. Tolerance 0.15 s. The ISO 18 tests add the grid of speeds and extinction coefficients that NIST suggests (the same values as ISO’s), and test_iso_table21_coupled.py reads K = 1.0 1/m from a committed FDS slice. NIST does not say where the time is measured; the 60 m stretch is our choice. The corridor runs with the SocialForceModel of assets/ISO-table21.

      Verif.2.6 is NIST’s room (10 m × 10 m × 3 m, no fire, one occupant at the centre held by a pre-evacuation time above 10 000 000 s) and the ISO 19 setup is the same, so the ISO 19 tests are the NIST tests; see ISO Test 19.

      Verif.4.1 differs in layout. NIST’s Figure 11 puts both 1 m exits on the 15 m walls near the far end and the occupant at the middle of the near 10 m wall; the ISO test as run here puts the occupant 5 m from each door. test_nist_verif_4_1_dynamic_exit.py builds NIST’s layout with the CollisionFreeSpeedModel. Figure 11 carries no dimension for the doors; Figure 10 (Verif.3.3, report p. 35) shows the same 10 m × 15 m room and the same occupant spot and labels the doors 12 m from the occupant’s wall, so the door centres are at y = 12 m, and the occupant starts within 0.5 m of (5 m, 1 m). Each door centre is √(5² + 11²) = 12.08 m from (5, 1). At the closure (1 s) the occupant has walked 1 m toward exit 1, to about (4.59, 1.91): 11.08 m from exit 1 and 11.45 m from exit 2. The route change is made at the closure and recorded at 1.0 s. The occupant leaves by exit 2 at about 1 + 11.45 = 12.45 s, asserted to ±1 s (the start area is 1 m × 1 m, so the path can differ by 0.5 m, and the exit counts at the door line, within the 0.2 m radius and the 1 m door depth); measured 12.17 s. Exit 1 usage is 0 of 1. The control without the closure leaves by exit 1 (hand value 12.08 s ± 1 s, measured 11.62 s).

      Verif.3.1Exit route allocationRun, passes (user-defined allocation)The ISO 8 test: 23 occupants in twelve rooms off a corridor, rooms 1–4 and 7–10 allocated to the main exit and the rest to the secondary one, as NIST states (test_iso_test_8_route_allocation.py). Differs from NIST’s Figure 8: the tested layout follows JuPedSim’s implementation. NIST’s rooms are 3.0 m × 5.0 m, the tested ones 2.8–2.9 m × 3.8 m; the corridor is 1.0 m wide in NIST and 0.9 m tested; the doors are 0.9 m against 0.8 m; room 3 is 2.0 m against 1.9 m; and NIST has a 1.0 m passage to the main exit that the tested layout does not reproduce. The layout was not rebuilt, so this row is a run of the ISO 8 layout, not of Figure 8.       Verif.3.2Social influenceOut of scopeHerding is not modelled (#78).
      Verif.3.3AffiliationRun, passes (ISO layout)The ISO 16 test: the cognitive-map model with the entrance exit known sends the occupant to exit 2 in 100 % of 11 runs, against 49.2 % of 61 runs without it (test_iso_test_16_familiar_exit.py). Differs from NIST: the room is the ISO 9 room, with exits about 5 m away and the occupant in the middle, not NIST’s Figure 10 layout (exits near the far end, occupant 1 m from the near wall), which was not run. The model has no graded preference between known exits, so this shows knowledge of an exit, not a probability weight.
      Verif.4.1Dynamic availability of exitsRun, passesNIST’s layout, details above (test_nist_verif_4_1_dynamic_exit.py). A schedule acts only through rerouting and needs routed agents.       Verif.5.1CongestionOut of scopeNIST’s geometry is a room, a corridor and a stair; there are no stairs.
      Verif.5.2Maximum flow ratesRun, passes (throttling on)The ISO 11 test: 100 occupants in an 8 m × 5 m room with a 1 m exit on the 5 m wall, as NIST states (test_iso_test_11_max_flow.py). NIST allows two readings. With exit throttling on at 1.33 p/m/s it is verification, and the measured flow stays at or below the limit. With throttling off (the default) the flow is emergent, a validation reading: JuPedSim’s CollisionFreeSpeedModel gives 3.78 p/m/s on the tested seed, reported and not asserted against 1.33 p/m/s. One layout.

      Where the list comes from

      The test list, geometries, scenarios and expected results are from the report (NIST TN 1822), Section 3.1. JuPedSim’s V&V suite maps the same 17 tests; its status, including its expected failures, is on the V&V standards coverage dashboard and applies to the models and versions it names, which may differ from the model and version a pyFDS-Evac scenario uses. The movement tests are inherited from JuPedSim and are not rerun here.
