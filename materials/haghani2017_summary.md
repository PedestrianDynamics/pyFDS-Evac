The paper "Stated and revealed exit choices of pedestrian crowd evacuees" by Haghani & Sarvi (*Transportation Research Part B* 95, 2017) directly interrogates the methodology that `[[lovreglio2014]]` and `[[lovreglio2016]]` (and Haghani's own earlier work) rely on: **is stated-preference (SP/SC) survey data â€” hypothetical choices in a video or picture â€” a reliable stand-in for how people actually choose exits in a real, physically competitive evacuation?** It answers this by collecting both kinds of data on the same variables and comparing the resulting models directly.

### 1. Two independent datasets, same four variables
*   **Stated-choice (SC), 4958 observations across 3 surveys:** face-to-face interviews at real public venues (two Melbourne train stations, one public building) using picture-based hypothetical scenarios of the venue the interviewee had just left. Variables: **DIST** (distance to exit), **CONG** (congestion at exit), **FLTOEX** (directional flow of evacuees toward the exit, split into visible/invisible exit versions **FLTOVIS**/**FLTOINVIS**), and **VIS** (whether the exit is visible from the decision point).
*   **Revealed-choice (RC), 3015 observations:** 150 real participants physically competed to escape a temporary, artificially built multi-exit room (2â€“4 exits, 75 or 150 people, varying exit widths) across 20 trial runs, tracked from an 8m-high camera using colour-tagged clothing and the **PeTrack** trajectory-extraction software. Each participant's actual "decision moment" (identified from head-orientation and trajectory-consistency cues) was extracted as one disaggregate choice observation â€” this is real physical behaviour under real competitive pressure, not a survey response.

### 2. Core finding: strong agreement on sign/significance, but a systematic SC-vs-RC bias
All four models (3 SC + 1 RC), fit as both Mixed Logit and Latent-Class Logit, agree that **DIST and CONG reduce exit utility and VIS increases it**, and all four are statistically significant â€” i.e. the basic behavioural mechanism is robust across data-collection method. However:
*   In every SC-derived model, **CONG's estimated marginal effect is stronger than DIST's**; in the RC (real) model, **this reverses** â€” DIST matters relatively more, CONG relatively less, than survey respondents' stated choices would suggest. This is a **systematic bias**, consistent across all three independently-run SC surveys, meaning survey-based exit-choice calibration (including `[[lovreglio2014]]`/`[[lovreglio2016]]`-style studies) likely **overweights crowd-avoidance relative to walking-distance** compared to real behaviour.
*   Despite this bias in the *coefficient scale*, the **predicted choice probabilities** from SC-only and RC-only models were still reasonably close in absolute terms (5â€“9% mean absolute difference), and cross-validated well against a jointly-estimated model â€” the paper's practical conclusion is that SC data remains usable when RC data is unavailable, but analysts should be aware it skews the DIST/CONG tradeoff.
*   **Latent-class analysis** reveals (in both SC and RC data) two distinct evacuee subpopulations: one whose primary decision driver is avoiding congestion, another primarily driven by exit visibility â€” heterogeneity that echoes the random-coefficient dispersion found in `[[lovreglio2016]]`'s NCDM/DIST variables.
*   For very large venues (train stations), the flow-of-other-evacuees factor (FLTOEX) did not reach significance â€” social-influence effects from moving crowds may be scale/context-dependent rather than universal.

### 3. Relevance to this repository
This is a direct methodological caution for any parameter in this codebase (e.g. `[[pyfds_evac/core/fed.py]]`'s susceptibility/threshold constants, or any future crowd/congestion cost term in routing) that traces its calibration back to a stated-preference survey like `[[lovreglio2014]]`/`[[lovreglio2016]]`: expect the *relative* weight of "avoid crowding" vs. "minimize distance" to be somewhat overstated relative to how people would actually behave in a real fire. If this project ever needs to pick between competing SP-survey-derived exit-choice coefficient sets, this paper is evidence for trusting the DIST term more, and the CONG/crowd term slightly less, than the raw survey numbers suggest.
